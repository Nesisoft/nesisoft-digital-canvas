Many schools in Ghana still run on exercise books, Excel sheets and WhatsApp groups. It works until it doesn't: when fees go unchased because nobody can see who owes what, when terminal reports are late because scores are scattered across a dozen sheets, or when a parent only hears about a problem at the end of term.

The right school management system fixes that, and gives staff back hours every week. The wrong one creates double work and gets abandoned by second term. This guide covers what to look for, whether you're a single school or a group of campuses.

## Start with your problems, not the features

Before you look at any software, write down what's going wrong today. For most schools, it's some of these:

- Fees tracked by hand, with arrears that are hard to follow up
- Terminal reports compiled late from scattered score sheets
- Parents who don't know what's happening until it's too late
- Records that live in one person's head, or one person's laptop
- Leadership with no clear view of enrolment, fees or performance

Then judge every system by how well it solves your list. A long feature list matters much less than whether staff will actually use it.

## The checklist

### 1. Student records and admissions

One record for each learner, from admission to completion: personal details, guardians and contacts, class history, fees, results and attendance. Look for easy promotion of whole classes at the end of the year, and siblings linked to the same parent, which helps with sibling discounts and with contacting families.

### 2. Fees, billing and mobile money

This is where good software pays for itself. Look for:

- Fee items set per class and per term, with discounts and scholarships
- Arrears carried forward automatically
- Part-payments, so parents can pay in instalments
- Payment by mobile money and card, with each payment matched to the right learner and a receipt sent automatically
- Reports for the bursar: what's been collected, what's outstanding, and by which class

Watch out for systems where staff still have to match MoMo payments by hand. "Received GH₵850 from 024 XXX XXXX" doesn't say which child it's for. Our guide to [accepting mobile money payments](/blog/accept-mobile-money-payments-ghana) explains how automatic matching works.

### 3. Assessment and terminal reports

Teachers enter class scores and exam scores, and the system works out totals, grades and positions, in your school's grading scale and weighting. Terminal reports should come out in your format, with the head's and class teacher's remarks, ready to print or send to parents. Check that the system copes when the weighting or grading scale changes, because it will.

### 4. Attendance and timetables

Quick daily registers that teachers can take on a phone, with patterns that are easy to spot, such as a learner who's absent every Monday. Timetables that show clashes before they happen.

### 5. Parent communication

Not every parent has a smartphone, and not every parent will download an app. SMS reaches everyone, so make sure it's built in: fee reminders, results, absence alerts, and notices about PTA meetings or closing dates. A parent portal or app is a good addition for the families who'll use it, not a replacement for SMS.

### 6. Roles and permissions

The proprietor, the head, teachers and the bursar each need different access. A teacher should see their own classes, not the school's finances. The bursar should manage fees but not change results. Good role controls protect learners' data and stop costly mistakes.

### 7. Reports for leadership

Enrolment by class, fee collection against target, arrears, and results over time. If you run more than one campus, look for reports across all of them as well as for each one.

### 8. Works on phones and weak connections

Teachers will enter scores on their phones, often on slow mobile data. Test the system on an ordinary Android phone, not just on the office computer. It's worth asking what happens if the connection drops halfway through entering a class's scores.

## Data protection is not optional

A school holds a lot of personal data about children and their families: names, dates of birth, contacts, health notes, fees and results. In Ghana, the Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843) applies, and the Data Protection Commission requires organisations that collect or process personal data to register with it.

Before you choose a system, ask:

- Where is our data stored, and who at the vendor can see it?
- How is it backed up, and how quickly could we recover it?
- Can we export all our data, in a standard format, at any time?
- What happens to our data if we stop using the system?
- Is there a record of who changed what, such as a result edited after it was published?

## Off-the-shelf or custom-built?

**Off-the-shelf software** is quick to start with, and usually priced per learner per term or per year. It suits schools whose processes are fairly standard. The risks are a loose fit with how you work, and costs that grow with every new learner. Check the export options before you commit.

**Custom-built software** fits your fee structure, your report format and your campuses exactly, and you own it. It costs more up front. Some schools start with off-the-shelf software and move to a custom system once they know exactly what they need. That works well, as long as you can take your data with you.

## Ten questions to ask before you sign

1. Can we try it with our own data for a few weeks?
2. Will it produce our terminal reports in our format?
3. How are mobile money fee payments matched to learners?
4. Is SMS included, and what does each message cost?
5. Does it work well on an ordinary Android phone?
6. Who can see what, and can we set that ourselves?
7. Where is our data stored, and can we export all of it?
8. What training do you provide for teachers and the bursar?
9. What does support cost, and how quickly do you respond?
10. What will we pay in total over three years, not just in the first term?

## Plan the switch around the school calendar

Basic schools in Ghana run three terms, so plan to go live at the start of a term, ideally the first. Set the system up and import your learners from Excel during the vacation. Train staff before reopening, not during the first week back.

Start with one or two areas, often fees and terminal reports, because that's where the time savings show first. Add the rest once staff are comfortable.
