Many businesses in Ghana already sell online. They post products on Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Status, take orders in chats and collect payment by MoMo. It works, until the orders grow and keeping track of them takes over the day.

That's usually when an online store starts to make sense. This guide covers the main ways to sell online, how to take payments and handle delivery, and how to win the trust of customers who've never bought from you before.

## Three ways to sell online

### 1. Social media and WhatsApp

Selling through WhatsApp Business, Instagram or TikTok is free to start and puts you where your customers already are.

**The catch:** every order is handled by hand. Someone answers each message, confirms the price, checks the MoMo payment came in and writes down the delivery details. Mistakes creep in as volume grows, and you have no clear record of who bought what.

### 2. Marketplaces

Online marketplaces give you access to shoppers who are already looking to buy.

**The catch:** you pay commission, you compete with other sellers on the same page, often on price, and the customer belongs to the marketplace more than to you.

### 3. Your own online store

Your own store gives you control of your brand, your prices and your customer relationships. Orders, payments and stock are tracked automatically, and you build a list of customers you can sell to again.

**The catch:** it costs money to build, and you have to bring visitors to it yourself.

Most growing businesses use all three: social media and WhatsApp to reach people, and their own store as the place orders are placed, paid for and tracked.

## Ways to build your own store

**Hosted store builders** let you set up a shop quickly for a monthly fee. They suit small catalogues and standard ways of selling. Check carefully that they support mobile money in Ghana and the delivery options you need.

**WordPress with WooCommerce** is free to start, flexible, and supported by Ghanaian payment providers such as Paystack. It needs hosting, regular updates and someone to look after it.

**A custom-built store** fits your business exactly: your delivery zones and fees, mobile money flows, instalment payments, wholesale prices for business customers, or several branches sharing one stock system. It costs more up front, but it does exactly what you need and you own it.

## Payments customers trust

Customers in Ghana expect to pay with mobile money (MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash and AT Money) and by card. Payment should be confirmed automatically and matched to the order, with a receipt sent straight away. Our guide to [accepting mobile money payments](/blog/accept-mobile-money-payments-ghana) explains how this works.

What about payment on delivery? Many customers prefer it, especially with a shop they don't know yet. It builds trust, but it also brings more failed deliveries and cash to manage. Common middle grounds:

- A small deposit by MoMo when ordering, with the balance paid on delivery
- Payment by MoMo at the door, before the parcel is handed over
- Payment on delivery for returning customers only

## Delivery that works

Delivery is where many online stores win or lose customers.

- **Set clear zones and fees.** Customers should see the delivery fee before they pay, not after.
- **Collect good directions.** Ask for a GhanaPostGPS digital address and a note about landmarks, and confirm by phone when needed.
- **Choose your riders carefully.** Your own riders give you control. Delivery companies help you reach other cities and regions.
- **Keep customers informed.** An SMS or WhatsApp message when the order is confirmed, sent out and delivered saves dozens of "where is my order?" calls.

## Winning trust

A new customer has to trust you with their money before they've seen your product. Help them:

- Use real photos of your products, not just pictures from the supplier.
- Show prices in cedis, including delivery, with no surprises at checkout.
- Publish a clear returns and refund policy.
- Show a phone number and a WhatsApp contact, and answer quickly.
- Collect and show reviews from real customers.
- Make sure the site loads securely (the padlock in the browser).

## Built for phones

Most of your visitors will be on phones, often on mobile data. A good mobile store has fast pages and compressed images, large buttons, a short checkout, and lets customers buy without creating an account first.

## Getting found

- Set up a free Google Business Profile so you show up on Google Maps and local searches.
- Write clear product names and descriptions that use the words customers search for.
- Share product links on WhatsApp, Instagram and TikTok, so social media brings people to your store.
