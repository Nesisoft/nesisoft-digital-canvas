AI coding tools have changed how software is built. Clients now ask us, fairly: "If AI writes the code, why should I pay full price?"

The honest answer is that AI does make many projects cheaper and faster, sometimes by a lot. But it doesn't speed up everything, and the biggest savings come with a trade-off you should understand before you choose. Here's how it works.

## What AI actually speeds up

In our work, AI makes the biggest difference to:

- **Routine code.** Forms, screens, API endpoints and the plumbing every app needs. AI drafts it, and engineers review it.
- **Tests.** AI suggests test cases, including edge cases people forget, and helps write the automated tests that run before every release.
- **Documentation.** Technical docs and handover guides get drafted as we go, not left to the end.
- **Early design.** Layout options and variations come together quickly, so you see more choices before anything is built.
- **Old code.** Understanding, fixing and upgrading a system nobody has touched in years is much faster.

## What it doesn't speed up

Some of the work that decides whether a project succeeds isn't typing code at all:

- **Deciding what to build.** Understanding your business, your users and what matters most.
- **Architecture, security and data design.** These decisions are hard to change later, and they need experienced people.
- **Other people's systems.** Payment providers, SMS gateways and government systems each have their own quirks and approval processes.
- **Testing in the real world.** Real phones, real networks, real mobile money prompts.
- **Waiting.** For app store review, for content, and for decisions and sign-offs.

That's why AI doesn't make every project ten times cheaper. It shrinks the coding part of a project, and the rest takes as long as it always did.

## Three ways to build

We offer three delivery models. Here's how they compare on a typical medium-sized mobile app for Android and iOS, using our pricing at the time of writing:

Put simply, an AI-assisted team currently costs about 30% less than a traditional one, and an AI-native team about half. You can switch between the three in our [app cost calculator](/app-cost-calculator) and see the difference for your own project.

### Traditional team

Experienced people do the work in sprints, and you can change your mind as you go. It's the familiar way, and it suits projects where flexibility matters most and AI tools aren't welcome, such as some regulated or confidential work.

### AI-assisted team

The same process, the same people, the same freedom to change direction. AI takes on the routine work, so engineers spend their time on the hard parts. For most projects this is the best balance, and it's what we recommend when clients aren't sure.

### AI-native team

The biggest saving, with one important condition: we agree a detailed specification with you before building starts. The screens, the features, the rules. A small team then builds it quickly with AI. Changes after sign-off are possible, but they're quoted separately.

The specification is the trade-off. AI-native works well when you know what you want. It works badly when you're still working it out, because every change of direction costs time and money.

## Which should you choose?

AI-native is a good fit when:

- You're building something well understood, such as a booking system, an online store or an internal tool
- You're replacing an existing system whose behaviour is already known
- You want a first version with a fixed scope, at the lowest cost
- One person on your side can make decisions quickly

AI-assisted or traditional is a better fit when:

- You're still exploring the idea, and you expect to learn and change as you go
- Several departments or partners need to agree on what gets built
- The project depends on systems or approvals outside your control
- The product will keep growing for years with a team behind it

## What doesn't change, whichever you pick

**People make the decisions.** Architecture, security and trade-offs are decided by engineers, not tools.

**Everything is reviewed and tested.** AI-drafted code goes through the same code review and testing as any other code before it ships.

**Your data stays private.** We keep confidential data, customer records and credentials out of AI prompts, and we're happy to sign an NDA first.

**You get normal code.** Clean, documented code that any developer can work with, and that you own.

**Running costs stay the same.** Hosting, app store accounts, payment fees and maintenance cost the same however the app was built.

## Questions to ask any developer who uses AI

Plenty of agencies now say they use AI. These questions separate those who use it well from the rest:

1. Who reviews the code AI writes, and how?
2. What testing happens before each release?
3. Does our data or source code go into AI tools, and under what terms?
4. Who owns the code at the end?
5. How are changes priced once the work has started?
6. Can we see something you've delivered this way?

## How to get the biggest saving

- **Write down what you want.** Who the users are, what they need to do, and what "done" looks like. Even a page of notes helps.
- **Bring examples.** Apps you like, forms you use today, reports you need. They save hours of back-and-forth.
- **Name one decision-maker.** Quick answers keep a fast team fast.
- **Have your content ready.** Text, images and price lists should arrive before they're needed.
