"How much will it cost?" is the first question almost everyone asks us. The honest answer is "it depends", but that's no help when you're trying to set a budget or convince a board.

So this guide uses real numbers. They're the price ranges we use at Nesisoft as of November 2026, and the same ones behind our [app cost calculator](/app-cost-calculator). You'll also see what makes a project cost more or less, three worked examples, and the costs that carry on after launch.

## The short answer

| What you're building | Starting from | Typical medium-sized build | Typical timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Business website | GH₵4,300 | GH₵6,800–10,000 | 2–4 weeks |
| Online store | GH₵11,000 | GH₵17,000–26,000 | 5–8 weeks |
| Web app or portal | GH₵16,000 | GH₵26,000–39,000 | 6–11 weeks |
| Mobile app (Android and iOS) | GH₵22,000 | GH₵34,000–52,000 | 8–13 weeks |
| Mobile app and web platform | GH₵33,000 | GH₵51,000–78,000 | 11–19 weeks |

"Starting from" is a small build with a ready-made design. "Typical" is a medium-sized build with a custom design. Neither includes extra features such as payments or chat, which come next.

These figures are a guide, not a quote, and prices change over time. For a range that fits your project, use the [calculator](/app-cost-calculator). It also shows prices in US dollars.

## What drives the price

### 1. What you're building

A website mostly presents information. An online store adds products, a cart, a checkout and orders. A web app is something people sign in to and use, such as a customer portal or a school system. A mobile app has to work on both Android and iOS and pass app store review. Each step up means more screens, more logic and more testing.

Not sure which one you need? Read [Website, web app or mobile app: which does your business need?](/blog/website-web-app-or-mobile-app)

### 2. Size

Size is mostly the number of screens and the number of kinds of user.

- **Small:** up to about 10 screens and one kind of user.
- **Medium:** about 10–25 screens and a couple of user types, such as customers and staff.
- **Large:** 25+ screens, several user types and detailed workflows. Expect roughly 60% more than a medium build.

### 3. Features

Every feature is designed, built and tested. As a guide, here is roughly what some common features add to a medium-sized build at the time of writing:

- User accounts and sign-in: around GH₵4,000
- Mobile money and card payments: around GH₵6,000
- SMS, email and push notifications: around GH₵3,000
- Reports and analytics: around GH₵6,000
- Admin dashboard: around GH₵8,000
- Maps and live location: around GH₵7,000
- In-app chat: around GH₵9,000
- Works offline: around GH₵8,000

Features are where budgets grow quietly. A list of "small extras" can add up to more than the app itself.

### 4. Design

A ready-made design adapted to your brand costs about 15% less than a custom design. A premium design, with custom illustrations and animation, costs about 25% more. For an internal tool your staff use every day, a clean ready-made design is often all you need.

### 5. Deadline

A flexible timeline lets us schedule your project around others, which saves a little. A hard deadline means more people working in parallel. That finishes about a quarter sooner, but it costs about 25% more.

### 6. How it's built

AI tools now draft a lot of routine code, tests and documentation. On our current pricing, an AI-assisted team costs about 30% less than a traditional team, and an AI-native team about half, each with trade-offs. We explain those in [AI-assisted development: what it means for your budget and timeline](/blog/ai-assisted-development-budget-and-timeline).

## Costs that carry on after launch

The build is only part of the bill. Plan for these as well:

- **Hosting and your domain.** Servers, the database and your web address, paid monthly or yearly to the provider.
- **App store accounts.** Google Play charges a one-off US$25 to register. Apple's developer programme costs US$99 a year.
- **Payment and SMS fees.** Mobile money, card and SMS providers charge per transaction or per message.
- **Maintenance.** Security fixes, updates for new phones and browsers, and improvements. Many businesses budget around 15–20% of the build cost each year.

## How to get more for your budget

**Launch a smaller first version.** Build the features your users can't do without, launch, then add the rest once real people are using it. You'll spend less up front and make better decisions about what to build next.

**Start on the web.** A web app works on every phone and computer without an install. Many products can prove themselves on the web before paying for Android and iOS apps.

**Have your content ready.** Text, photos, product lists and price lists that arrive late hold up a project as surely as code does.

**Make decisions quickly.** A week waiting for sign-off is a week the team can't move forward.

**Compare quotes carefully.** A very low price often leaves something out. Ask every developer:

- Is testing on real Android and iOS phones included?
- Who owns the source code and the accounts when the project ends?
- What happens after launch, and what does support cost?
- How are changes to the scope priced?
