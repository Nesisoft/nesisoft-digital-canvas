Not every business needs an app in the App Store. Many need something that works like an app, sits on the phone's home screen and opens instantly, without asking customers to download anything first. That's what a progressive web app, or PWA, offers.

## What a PWA is

A progressive web app is a website built to behave like an app. People open it from a link, as with any website. If they like it, they can add it to their home screen, and from then on it:

- opens from its own icon, full screen, without the browser's address bar
- loads quickly, because it keeps the files it needs on the phone
- can keep working without a connection, for the parts designed to
- can send notifications, once the user agrees

Behind the scenes it's one web app that works on Android, iPhone and computers alike.

## Why PWAs suit Ghana

**Nothing to download first.** Asking people to download an app before they've seen any value is a big hurdle, especially when data and phone storage are tight. A PWA opens from a link, so the first visit costs a few hundred kilobytes, not a large download.

**Easy to share.** A link travels easily on WhatsApp, in an SMS or on social media. An app store listing doesn't.

**Found on Google.** The public pages of a PWA appear in search results like any website. App store apps don't.

**Always up to date.** Every visitor gets the latest version immediately. There's no app store review and no waiting for people to update.

**One build for every device.** One codebase covers Android, iPhone, tablets and computers, which usually makes a PWA cheaper to build and maintain than a mobile app.

## What a PWA can and can't do in 2026

Browsers can do a lot more than most people think, but there are still differences between Android and iPhone.

| Feature | Android | iPhone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add to home screen | Yes, and the site can offer a button to install | Yes, through Share → Add to Home Screen |
| Works offline | Yes, for what it's built to do | Yes, for what it's built to do |
| Notifications | Yes | Only once added to the home screen |
| Camera and location | Yes, while the app is open | Yes, while the app is open |
| Payments, including mobile money | Yes, through a payment provider's checkout | Yes, through a payment provider's checkout |
| Listed in the app store | Can be published on Google Play | No |

Some things still need a native app: location tracking in the background, close work with Bluetooth devices, heavy graphics, and anything that must run when the app is closed.

On iPhone, there's no automatic install prompt, so many people won't know they can add your PWA to their home screen. Show them how with a short, friendly instruction when they're on an iPhone.

Browsers can also clear data a site has stored if it isn't used for a while. Keep anything important on your server, and use the phone only to save work in progress until it's sent.

## Designing for weak connections

Working offline isn't automatic. You decide which parts of the app keep working without a connection, and how:

- **Cache the essentials,** such as the app's screens, your logo and recently viewed items, so the app opens even with no signal.
- **Queue actions.** If a user submits a form offline, save it on the phone and send it when the connection returns, without creating duplicates.
- **Show the status.** Tell people when they're offline and what will happen to their work.
- **Keep it light.** Small pages and compressed images help everyone, offline or not.

## PWA or mobile app?

A PWA is a strong choice when:

- people use it occasionally, not every day
- you want customers to find it on Google and share it on WhatsApp
- your budget is tight and you want one build for every device
- you want to test an idea before investing in app store apps

A mobile app is the better choice when:

- people use it daily and you rely on notifications, especially on iPhone
- you need background location, Bluetooth or heavy graphics
- being in the App Store and Google Play matters to your customers
