AI is no longer just for big technology companies. The same tools behind the chat assistants everyone has heard of are available to any business, either as ready-made products or as services that can be built into your own website, app or system.

The businesses that get real value from AI aren't chasing it. They start with a problem that costs them time or money, and they use AI where it clearly helps. This guide covers where that usually is, how to start, and what to watch out for.

## Where AI helps today

### Answering customers' questions

Many businesses answer the same questions all day: prices, opening hours, delivery areas, how to pay, what documents to bring. An AI assistant on your website, in your app or on WhatsApp can answer them at any hour, and pass the conversation to a person when it can't help.

The key is to ground it in your own information, such as your price list, policies and FAQs, so it answers from what you've given it and doesn't make things up.

### Reading documents and forms

Invoices, receipts, delivery notes, application forms and ID documents all have to be typed into a system by someone. AI can read a photo or a PDF and fill in the details, leaving staff to check and correct rather than type everything from scratch.

### Writing and summarising

Product descriptions, first drafts of letters and reports, and summaries of long documents or meeting notes are all quicker with AI. Staff still review the result, but they start from a draft instead of a blank page.

AI handles English best. Support for Ghanaian languages such as Twi, Ewe and Ga is improving, but it's less reliable. If your customers use them, test with native speakers before relying on AI to read or write them.

### Finding answers in your own information

Staff spend a surprising amount of time looking for things: a policy, a procedure, the terms of a contract, what was agreed with a customer last year. AI can search across your documents and answer questions in plain language, with a link to the source so people can check.

### Spotting patterns

AI and older statistical tools can summarise sales, flag unusual transactions, forecast which stock will run low, and highlight customers who've gone quiet. The output is a prompt for a person to look closer, not a decision on its own.

## How to start

**1. Pick one problem.** Choose something specific and measurable: "We spend 20 hours a week typing invoices" or "Customers wait hours for answers on WhatsApp."

**2. Try ready-made tools first.** Many problems can be tested with existing products before anything is built. You'll learn quickly whether AI helps.

**3. Build it into your systems when it works.** The real gains come when AI is part of the tools your staff already use, such as your customer portal, your admin dashboard or your app, rather than a separate website people copy and paste between.

**4. Keep a person in the loop.** Let AI draft, sort and suggest. Let people approve anything that matters: payments, prices, refunds, credit, hiring, medical or legal information.

**5. Measure the result.** Compare time spent, response times or errors before and after. Keep what works and drop what doesn't.

## What it costs

Ready-made AI tools are usually priced per user per month. AI services built into your own software are usually charged by how much they're used, which is modest for most small and medium businesses, though worth monitoring as use grows.

The larger cost is usually the work of connecting AI to your own information and systems, and designing it so staff trust it. Start with a small, well-defined project, and grow from there.

## Risks to manage

**Confident wrong answers.** AI can produce answers that sound right and aren't. Ground it in your own information, show sources where you can, and keep people checking anything important.

**Customer data.** Don't paste customer records, ID numbers or confidential information into consumer AI tools. Use business services with clear terms on how data is handled, and remember your obligations under Ghana's Data Protection Act, 2012 (Act 843).

**Fairness.** If AI helps with decisions about people, such as loans or job applications, check that it treats people fairly, and keep a person responsible for the decision.

**Depending on one supplier.** AI services change their prices and products often. Build so you can switch providers without starting again.

## Good first projects

- A WhatsApp or website assistant that answers a clinic's or school's common questions and books appointments
- Reading supplier invoices into a distributor's stock and accounts system
- A daily summary of sales, stock and unpaid invoices for a shop owner, sent every morning
- Searching a company's policies and procedures for new staff
